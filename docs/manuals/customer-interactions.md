# Customer interactions and response guide

[All manuals](START-HERE.md) · [Administrator](administrator.md) · [Salesperson](salesperson.md) · [Cleaner](cleaner.md)

This guide explains the actual customer journey and suggested staff responses. Sample replies are drafts to personalize, not automatic messages or company promises. Send them through the business's approved channel. The current preview does not send real customer email.

## 1. What the customer can do

**Public quote request:** the customer enters name, email, phone, address, residential/commercial type, services of interest, optional preferred date and notes, and consent to quote-related contact. The site confirms receipt and saves a lead. This is not an instant quote or appointment confirmation.

**Shared estimate link:** no account is required. The customer sees the scope, quantities/prices, total, terms, and any expiration. They can download a PDF, enter their full name and check the agreement box to approve, or expand the decline section and submit an optional reason. The resulting page confirms their decision and says staff will follow up about scheduling.

**Customer portal:** a linked account can see its estimates, upcoming appointments, invoices/payments, completed service history, and request another service at a listed property. Account setup/linking currently needs office administration; submitting a public quote does not create a login automatically.

**Invoice link:** the customer can review the invoice, payments, balance, payment instructions, and PDF. There is no online Pay now/checkout feature. Payment is arranged outside the website and recorded by the office.

**Repeat service request:** the customer chooses a linked property and describes the work/timing. The site acknowledges the request and creates a new lead. It does not automatically confirm the price, book the visit, or rehire the previous cleaner.

## 2. Interaction playbook

### New quote request

**Owner:** assigned salesperson; administrator assigns unclaimed leads.

**Action in the site:** open **Leads**, read the request, check duplicates and contact preferences, clarify scope, then update notes/status/follow-up.

**Suggested reply:** “Thanks for contacting FirstGlanceKnox, [name]. I have your request for [service] at [property]. Could you confirm [missing detail]? We'll use that to prepare a clear estimate. Your requested date is [date]; we'll confirm availability before booking.”

**Record next:** missing information, next contact date, and who is preparing the estimate.

### Customer asks for a price or discount

**Owner:** salesperson prepares; administrator approves exceptions.

**Action:** build the estimate from the price book. Ask the administrator about an unapproved discount or service exception.

**Suggested reply:** “I'll confirm the service scope and send a written estimate showing the total. I'll check with the office about [requested adjustment] before including it.”

**Avoid:** treating demo prices as approved business prices or promising a discount before review.

### Estimate sent; customer needs help approving

**Owner:** salesperson or administrator.

**Suggested reply:** “Open your estimate link and review the work and total. If everything looks right, enter your full name, check the agreement box, and select Approve estimate. You can download a PDF there too. Approval records the scope; we'll confirm the appointment separately.”

**If it fails:** ask them to refresh the link, read any displayed error, and report the estimate number/time to the office. If the link is expired/revoked, the office must check its status and provide an appropriate replacement. Do not ask for their password. A `127.0.0.1` preview link cannot be used from their separate device.

### Estimate approved

**Owner:** salesperson or administrator books; administrator staffs the job.

**Action:** verify Accepted in **Estimates**, coordinate a time, **Schedule this job**, and hand off for cleaner offers.

**Suggested reply:** “Thank you—your approval for [estimate number] is recorded. We can review [proposed time] for your visit. We'll confirm the appointment and any access or preparation details with you.”

**Record next:** booked job number, agreed time, access requirements, and assignment owner. Do not call it fully staffed until the needed cleaner acceptance is in place.

### Customer declines or requests a revision

**Owner:** salesperson, with administrator support.

**Action:** review the response, record the requested change, and coordinate a corrected estimate. For an unaccepted estimate, use **Revise estimate**, record the reason, then review and send the replacement. Old links are retired. Accepted agreements remain unchanged and require an office-led change process.

**Suggested reply:** “Thanks for letting us know. Would you like us to revisit [scope/timing/specific concern]? If so, we'll prepare updated details for you to review before any booking.”

**If they are done:** thank them and close the follow-up appropriately. Record Do not contact if requested. Sending another copy of a declined estimate does not make it open for approval again.

### Appointment confirmation or preparation question

**Owner:** administrator or coordinating salesperson.

**Action:** verify actual booking and staffing, read the scope/access notes, and confirm only the preparation required for that job.

**Suggested reply:** “Your appointment is confirmed for [date/time, Knoxville time] at [property], for [approved scope]. Please [agreed preparation]. If access or your plans change, contact the office so we can update the appointment.”

**Avoid:** inventing arrival guarantees or asking the customer to perform preparation that was not agreed for their property.

### Reschedule or cancellation request

**Owner:** administrator. Salespeople/cleaners relay the request.

**Action:** confirm the affected job, requested time and reason. Use **Reschedule appointment** or **Cancel appointment**. Rescheduling requires new cleaner offers and acceptance. Communicate the result separately.

**Suggested reply:** “I have your request to [change/cancel] the visit on [date]. The office will check the appointment and crew availability, update the schedule, and confirm the outcome with you.”

**After the change succeeds:** “Your visit has been [rescheduled to date/time / canceled]. [Confirmed next step].” Do not send this final wording before the record and arrangements actually match it.

### Cleaner cannot gain access or finds an issue

**Owner:** cleaner reports; administrator coordinates resolution.

**Action:** enter issue details using **Report an issue**, contact the office, and document relevant private evidence. The administrator decides whether to rearrange the visit or address the scope.

**Suggested reply:** “We encountered [specific access/problem] and need to confirm the next step with the office before continuing that part of the work. I'll make sure they have the details.”

**Record next:** affected area, attempted contact, actual work completed, and what remains. Do not report the whole job completed when work remains unresolved.

### Customer asks the cleaner for extra work

**Owner:** cleaner records; office/salesperson confirms scope and price.

**Suggested reply:** “I can pass that request to the office so they can confirm the scope, price, and time needed. I'll continue with the work already agreed while they review it, if that remains appropriate.”

**Action:** note the request and obtain an office decision. Do not promise that the existing price or fixed job pay includes it.

### Completion concern or complaint

**Owner:** cleaner documents immediate observations; administrator owns resolution.

**Action:** record the customer's specific concern, location, and relevant private photos. If work is awaiting approval, the administrator can **Return for correction**. If already completed/invoiced, escalate for an office-led correction plan; there is no general completed-job reopening workflow.

**Suggested reply:** “Thank you for pointing out [specific concern]. I'll record it and have the office review it with the crew. We'll confirm the next step with you.”

**Avoid:** arguing, promising a refund, or changing invoice amounts without administrator review.

### Invoice sent or payment question

**Owner:** administrator.

**Action:** review the customer invoice/PDF, verify delivery, and explain the displayed payment instructions. Verify receipts before **Record payment**.

**Suggested reply:** “Your invoice [number] shows [verified balance] due by [date]. You can review the services and download the PDF using your invoice link. Please use the payment arrangements confirmed by the office and include the invoice number.”

**If the customer says they already paid:** “Thanks. Please share the payment date, method, and reference so the office can match it to your invoice.” Do not request full card or bank credentials. Do not mark paid based only on the statement.

**For a partial payment:** confirm the verified receipt and remaining balance after it has been recorded. For disputed charges/refunds, have the office review both the payment provider and the ledger; a website reversal alone does not refund money.

### Customer requests another visit

**Owner:** assigned salesperson or administrator.

**Action:** find the new lead, review the prior property/service history, and confirm current scope and availability before estimating or booking.

**Suggested reply:** “Thanks for requesting another clean at [property]. Is the scope the same as last time, and are there any changes to access or timing? We'll confirm the service details and appointment with you.”

For a recurring plan, confirm that a real future job exists and is staffed. Saving a recurring plan is not proof that its background generation ran.

## 3. What the website does versus what staff do

| Event | Website behavior | Staff responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quote/repeat request | Saves request/lead and displays acknowledgment | Review, assign, contact, clarify, follow up |
| Send estimate | Creates access link and queues delivery | Check recipient, delivery, and customer understanding |
| Customer approves/declines | Saves decision; shows acknowledgment | Review decision and coordinate next action |
| Booking/change | Validates and saves appointment state | Confirm details with customer and staff the job |
| Cleaner offer | Saves terms and creates in-app update | Monitor response; replace unanswered/declined offers |
| Completion submission | Saves work for review | Review and approve/return; resolve concerns |
| Invoice/payment | Displays bill; records office-entered receipts | Actually deliver invoice, receive/match funds, answer disputes |
| Mark compensation paid | Records payout reference | Make/verify payment outside the site |

## 4. Handoff note template

Copy this structure into the relevant lead/job notes or an approved internal handoff:

> Record number: [lead/estimate/job/invoice]
>
> Customer request: [what they asked]
>
> Confirmed facts: [scope, date, access, payment reference as appropriate]
>
> Response given: [what was actually communicated]
>
> Next action and owner: [person + task]
>
> Follow-up: [date/time or triggering event]

Use private lead/job notes for staff coordination. Customer-facing estimate notes and terms are visible to the customer. Keep staff compensation and internal disputes out of customer documents.
