# Salesperson instruction manual

[All manuals](START-HERE.md) · [Customer response guide](customer-interactions.md)

Your job is to turn a customer conversation into a clear, approved scope and an orderly handoff. You can work your authorized leads and estimates and book approved work. Administrators control the price book, assignment offers, schedule changes, invoices, and payment records.

## 1. Start your day

Open **Overview**, then **Notifications** and **Leads**. Review assigned requests, due follow-ups, and customer estimate responses. Use **Customers** to review the properties associated with your authorized records. Check **Schedule** before discussing availability.

An empty list may mean you have no assigned records. Ask the office to assign a lead instead of creating a duplicate to gain access.

## 2. Record a new doorstep conversation

1. Select **Quick lead** on the phone bar or **＋ New lead** on Overview.
2. Enter **Service address**, customer/business name if known, property type, and available contact details.
3. Choose the outcome: Interested, No answer, Follow up later, Estimate requested, Not interested, or Do not contact.
4. Add visit notes and a follow-up time when needed.
5. Select **Save lead & visit**.
6. If matching address activity appears, read it first. Confirm a new visit only when it is actually needed.

Ask for the customer's preferred contact method and a useful time to follow up. Record access restrictions and scope questions. A lead is a record of the conversation, not a booking.

## 3. Follow up on an existing lead

1. Open **Leads**, search by customer/address/email, and select the lead.
2. Read the conversation, previous activity, and existing estimates.
3. Contact the customer through the approved external channel.
4. Under **Keep things moving**, update status, follow-up time, and notes; select **Update lead**.

Keep existing context when updating the notes field. Add a dated summary of the new conversation and next step. Do not put staff compensation or private office comments in customer-facing estimate notes.

If the customer says “Do not contact me,” record that outcome/status and stop routine outreach. Ask the administrator to handle any later request to reopen the record.

## 4. Build and share an estimate

1. From the lead, select **Build estimate**.
2. Select a service and quantity for each required row. Rates come from the price book; leave unused rows blank.
3. Enter only an authorized discount and business-approved configured tax rate.
4. Enter the expiration, customer notes, and terms. Be explicit about included work and access assumptions.
5. Select **Create estimate** and review the total with the scope.
6. If pricing review is required, ask the administrator to approve it. Do not promise the exception before approval.
7. Select **Send estimate** once it is ready. Share the displayed secure link through the approved communication method.

Explain: “You can review the scope and total, download the PDF, then type your name and approve. We'll confirm scheduling after that.” Sending the estimate is not customer acceptance, and local preview email is not external delivery.

If the customer wants a revision to an unaccepted estimate, open it and choose **Revise estimate**. Update the saved lines, add rows with **Add service line**, or mark unwanted lines **Delete**. Enter a reason and **Save revision**. The old version stays in history and its links are revoked. Review/send the replacement for fresh customer approval; pricing exceptions still require administrator approval. Accepted terms cannot be revised this way. Do not ask a customer to approve an incorrect scope with a verbal promise to fix it later.

## 5. Respond to customer decisions

**Accepted:** open the estimate and confirm its Accepted status. Contact the customer about a suitable appointment. Do not ask them to approve again merely because no job exists yet.

**Declined:** review their explanation where available and record follow-up details. Ask one clear question if a response is welcome: “Would you like us to revisit the scope, timing, or another detail?” Respect a request to stop. Use a revision with updated details to request a new decision; a new link to the old declined estimate does not reopen it.

**No response:** check that the customer has the correct usable link and that the estimate is still open. Follow the agreed follow-up schedule. The site does not have a built-in customer conversation inbox.

## 6. Book approved work and hand off

1. Open the accepted estimate and select **Schedule this job**.
2. Enter start and finish in Knoxville time. Review unavailable windows.
3. Select **Confirm appointment**. If the system reports a conflict, coordinate another time.
4. Give the administrator the job number and flag access, timing, and service requirements. The administrator must create cleaner offers.
5. Confirm customer expectations with the office before promising staffing or additional work.

If the estimate already has a job, open that job rather than booking it again. For a date change or cancellation, send the existing job number, requested change, and reason to the administrator. Salespeople cannot perform those changes in the current workspace.

## 7. Answer billing questions without changing billing

If the customer asks about an invoice, payment balance, refund, or receipt, send the question and relevant record number to the administrator. Do not mark a lead Won or a job complete as a substitute for recording payment. Do not tell the customer an invoice is paid until the office verifies it.

Check **My earnings** for your own commission entries. Pending commission becomes earned when the associated invoice is fully paid; the office marks it paid after the external payout. Cleaner pay, if you also work as a cleaner, appears separately.

## 8. Offline and end-of-day routine

Quick leads can be saved to the device outbox when offline. An already-open eligible job form can also queue status updates if you have Cleaner responsibilities. Wait for synchronization success before treating those updates as server records. Photos and customer documents require a connection.

Reconnect using the same account. Open **Device outbox** to review waiting updates and errors. Use **Retry synchronization** after reconnecting. If the office confirms an update is obsolete, choose **Discard this update** and confirm; this removes only the local queued copy, not a server record. The site blocks sign-out while updates remain. If a request may already have succeeded, check the server record before discarding or recreating it. Editing a rejected update still requires a fresh corrected form after resolving the old queued copy.

Before finishing, update lead outcomes and next follow-ups, hand off accepted estimates/bookings, report pending revisions, and resolve unsynced records.
